### ❌ What's wrong
1. **No specific first build.** "Build small existing robot toys" is a direction, not a project.
2. **No finish line,** so hobby projects drift and die quietly.
3. **The business angle is unclear,** if there is meant to be one. STEM robotics kits are a crowded, low-margin market in India.

### 🤔 Why it's wrong
- Hardware punishes vagueness. Every wrong guess costs money and shipping time, unlike software where you just undo.
- Without a concrete target you buy the wrong parts and stall at "LED blinks".

### 🔧 How to make it right
1. **Pick one build with a 4-week deadline.** For example, an **ESP32 Bluetooth car controlled from your phone** or a **line-follower robot**. The kit costs about ₹2–5k.
2. **Then one step up:** add a camera (ESP32-CAM) or obstacle avoidance.
3. **Film it for the YouTube idea:** *"Zero to robot in 30 days"* is a proven format.
4. **If you ever want a business:** don't sell kits (crowded). Look at niches like **robotics workshops for schools in Chennai** or **custom automation for small shops**. Only explore this after 3 completed builds.

### 🌍 The space
| Player type | Lesson |
|---|---|
| Indian STEM kit brands and school robotics labs (e.g. Atal Tinkering Labs) | Demand exists through schools. B2B is where the money is, not toys |
| Global hobby ecosystems (Arduino, ESP32, Raspberry Pi) | Free tutorials and parts are everywhere. Learning cost is low |
| Robotics YouTubers | A build journey makes great content |
