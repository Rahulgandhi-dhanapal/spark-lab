# Emotion-theme movie-clip Shorts channel (story subtitles + free music)

**The spark** (one or two lines)
A YouTube Shorts channel of 20-second collages from movies and series built around one strong feeling (love, breakup, cheating, rags to riches, revenge). Story subtitles on screen explain what is happening, music comes from the YouTube Audio Library, and each short ends with a question. Viewers get hooked and comment "which movie?" or answer the question.

**Why it's interesting**
1. Pain / want: people always watch this format; emotional themes stop the scroll and drive comments.
2. Buyer: no direct buyer. Income comes from YouTube ad share (once monetised), then shoutouts, affiliate links, and using the audience to promote my other products (Trailbite, review service).
3. Edge: low on its own. The edge comes from consistent theme packaging, good story subtitles, a fast AI-assisted pipeline, and later a Tamil angle.
4. Fit: solo, part-time, about 5 hours a week if the pipeline is automated.
5. Speed: a 4-week test is easy, with no money needed.
6. Watch-outs (honest):
   - Movie video is still copyrighted. Content ID matches the picture, so expect some **claims** (that short's money goes to the studio; channel is fine).
   - **Strikes** (manual takedowns) are rarer but 3 delete the channel. Avoid new or hot titles (new Netflix and K-drama releases are claimed most).
   - Pure clip + music is refused monetisation as "reused content". The story subtitles, the question, and my own take are what make it count as my own work.
   - Never use famous songs; only YouTube Audio Library or other licensed free music.
   - Keep Trailbite first; this is a side bet, not the 90-day income answer.

**Format rules for every short**
- 18 to 25 seconds, vertical 9:16, 3 to 4 short scenes from one film or series.
- First 2 seconds: the strongest emotional frame with a hook line ("She found out on their wedding day...").
- Subtitles tell the story and add a one-line take ("He chose money over her. Big mistake.") rather than just translating the dialogue.
- Music: YouTube Audio Library only.
- End card: a question ("Would you forgive him?") to drive comments.
- Prefer older or lesser-known films and series; log the source for every short.

**Pipeline for a coding agent to build (the "workforce")**
1. *Idea list*: a sheet of themes × titles (theme, film/series, scene timestamps, release year, risk note).
2. *Script step*: AI writes 3 hook lines + subtitle script (max ~50 words) + end question per short; I pick one.
3. *Cut step*: script cuts the timestamped scenes from a local video file I own or have access to, crops to 9:16 (e.g. ffmpeg).
4. *Subtitle step*: burns in styled subtitles from the script, with timing.
5. *Music step*: picks a track from a folder of downloaded YouTube Audio Library songs, by mood tag, and mixes it low under any dialogue.
6. *Output*: final MP4 + title, description and hashtags draft + a row added to the tracking sheet.
7. *Tracker*: per short, record views, % viewed vs swiped away, comments, new subscribers, and any claim or strike, pulled from YouTube Studio by hand or via the YouTube Analytics API.
Upload stays manual (I review each short before posting).

**First step, if I were to try it**
Fresh channel, one short a day for 28 days (25+ posted), same format every time.
- Pass: median 1,000+ views per short, best short 20,000+, 60%+ viewed vs swiped, 300+ new subscribers, 5+ comments per short, 0 strikes, under 90 min per short.
- Kill: median under 200 views, under 50 subscribers, any strike, or more than 3 hours per short.
- Any claim: note the film, drop that source.
If it passes, continue 4 more weeks and try a Tamil-cinema version and one long video.

Evaluation notes: idea filter scored the copied-clip version 3.5/10 and the safer version (own subtitles/take + free music) 6.5/10.
