# Apartment care: rainwater harvesting and rooftop solar maintenance

**The spark** (one or two lines)
An annual maintenance contract for apartment associations covering rainwater harvesting cleaning and audits, plus rooftop solar panel cleaning and output checks.

**Why it's interesting**
1. Rainwater harvesting has been mandatory in Tamil Nadu since 2003, but many systems are now clogged or broken, and Chennai swings between floods and water shortages.
2. The PM Surya Ghar scheme is pushing rooftop solar to 1 crore homes. Dusty panels lose output and almost nobody maintains them.
3. Recurring revenue, one buyer covers 50–200 homes, and it needs no technology to start.

**First step, if I were to try it**
Offer a free rainwater harvesting check to 3 apartment associations on OMR and see what we find.

### ❌ What's wrong
1. **Rainwater harvesting has invisible value.** Associations don't pay for something they can't see working until the borewell dries up or the basement floods.
2. **Solar maintenance may already be covered.** Under PM Surya Ghar, installers are expected to provide years of maintenance (around 5 years; check the current rules). Paying demand appears mostly after that, or from unhappy customers.
3. **Apartment associations decide slowly and push hard on price.** Committees, quotes, AGMs: a 2–3 month sales cycle for a few thousand rupees.
4. **It's labour-heavy.** Growth means hiring and managing field staff, which is a different skill from coding.

### 🤔 Why it's wrong
- The pitch sells **maintenance**, which people put off, instead of an **outcome** they care about: lower water-tanker bills, higher solar savings, no flooding.
- Without measurement, you can't prove value, so you compete on price with local plumbers.

### 🔧 How to make it right
1. **Sell the outcome with numbers:** "Your association spent ₹X on water tankers last summer. After our RWH fix, here is the borewell level and tanker count." For solar: "Your panels produced 14% less than expected. After cleaning, here's the gain."
2. **Use your tech edge:** a cheap borewell level sensor, solar output readings and a monthly WhatsApp report to the association. That's what separates you from a plumber.
3. **Time the sales:** pitch RWH in August and September, before the northeast monsoon (Oct–Dec in Chennai), and pitch solar cleaning in the dusty summer.
4. **Target large gated communities on OMR** (200+ flats): one sale, big contract.
5. **Partner with solar installers** to take over maintenance after their warranty period ends.

### 🌍 The space
| Player | What they do | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Rain Centre / local RWH consultants (Chennai) | Advice and audits, often non-profit | Good partners for credibility and training |
| Local plumbers | Cheap, ad-hoc fixes | You win on measurement and reporting, not price |
| Solar installers and their maintenance contracts | Bundled maintenance for the first years | Partner, then take over after year 5 |
| Facility management companies | Bundle everything for big complexes | Potential buyer or acquirer later |

**Next step:** one free RWH audit with photos and a 1-page report. If the association asks "how much to fix it?", you have a business.
