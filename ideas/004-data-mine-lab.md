## 🤖 Claude · VC review: Data Mine Lab

**Verdict: pass in its current form.** It's the riskiest idea on the board: legal exposure, strong incumbents, and a lot of capital needed.

| | Score |
|---|---|
| Problem is real | 7/10 (AI companies do need Indian data) |
| Market size | 8/10 |
| Feasibility for this team | 2/10 |
| Moat | 3/10 |
| Investable today | 1/10 |

### ❌ What's wrong
1. **"Collect unstructured data of Indian users and sell it to companies"** is exactly what India's **DPDP Act 2023** targets. Personal data needs consent tied to a specific purpose, and reselling it is high-risk.
2. **Buyers don't want raw unstructured data.** They want data that's **licensed, consented, cleaned, labelled and documented**. Making it that way is most of the work and most of the cost.
3. **There's no collection channel.** Where do the users and their data come from?
4. **Incumbents already do this well,** with funding and NGO and government partnerships.

### 🤔 Why it's wrong
- The idea treats data as something you *mine*. In 2026 it's something you **license with consent and pay for**. Scraped or unconsented data is a liability buyers avoid.
- A data business only defends itself through **exclusive access** to a source. The idea doesn't name one.

### 🔧 How to make it right
1. **Flip it to consent-first and paid:** contributors are paid per task and know exactly what their data is used for. That's the Karya model.
2. **Pick one narrow, under-served dataset.** For example, **Tamil food and recipe speech plus text** (dish names, regional vocabulary, spoken orders). It's specific, useful for voice and search models, and connects to your Nest knowledge.
3. **Or start as a service, not a data seller:** do **data labelling and annotation for Tamil and Indian-English** for AI startups. You earn money from day one with no data liability.
4. **Possible link to Nest:** anonymised, *opt-in* taste data could later be valuable, but only with clear consent, or it destroys the trust Nest depends on.

### 🌍 The space
| Player | What they do | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Karya** (Bengaluru, nonprofit) | Pays rural workers to create Indian-language data; clients include Microsoft and Google; workers get royalties when data is resold | The ethical, consent-first version already exists and has distribution |
| **AI4Bharat** (IIT Madras) | Open Indian-language datasets and models | A lot of basic data is free. You must offer something rarer |
| **Scale AI, Appen, etc.** | Global labelling at scale | Generic labelling is a price war. Go niche |

**Next step:** park it, or test option 3 by finding one AI startup that needs Tamil annotation and doing a small paid job.
